The five numbers an analyst should see before anything else. Everything below is scoped by the filter row above — these tiles move with it, so the headline and the charts can never disagree.

2Alerts (FDR-gated)feeds the notification service

The highest-value section: which cells are behaving unusually enough to act on, and the exact evidence behind each one.

How a candidate becomes an alert

This is the logic the notification service will consume, so it is spelled out rather than buried. Each cell's volume is tested against a Negative-Binomial baseline fitted on its own trailing window, giving an exceedance probability. Those p-values are corrected across every supported cell within the same day by Benjamini–Hochberg, and alert_flag is q < 0.05 — a calibrated false-discovery rate, not an arbitrary z threshold. Cells that fail the support gate are never tested at all, so they cannot be flagged.

Severity bands: ◆critical q < 0.001  ·  ▲high q < 0.01  ·  ●medium q < 0.05. Severity is evidence strength, not impact — a critical q-value on a four-post cell is strong evidence of a small thing. Colour never carries the severity alone: every chip pairs an icon with the word.

Alert candidates per day

Alert candidates

Sorted by severity, then by q-value. The rationale column is the human-readable trace of why the cell fired — it is carried in alert_candidates.rationale so the notification payload can quote it verbatim.

  • Volume surprise -log10 P(X >= V | NegBinom(mu_baseline * effort_t/effort_baseline, theta)) units: -log10 p (1.0 = p 0.1, 3.0 = p 0.001)
    replaces the v1 robust-z (defect D1). Baseline is fitted by MLE on the trailing 28 days EXCLUDING the current day, with a method-of-moments theta fallback and a Poisson fallback when theta is unidentifiable. Overdispersion is honest but self-masking: a baseline that already contains spikes inflates 1/theta and depresses surprise.
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • Exceedance p-value P(X >= V | fitted count baseline), exact discrete upper tail units: probability
    one-sided; only upward excursions are tested
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • FDR q-value (per day) Benjamini-Hochberg over all supported cells of the same day, on mid-p units: q-value
    mid-p (P(X>V)+0.5P(X=V)) is used for FDR because the exact discrete tail is conservative; surprise_p reports the exact value. m per day is small (<=~15 supported cells), so power is limited — surprise_q_global applies BH across the whole panel instead.
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • Intensity alert surprise_q < 0.05 units: boolean
    calibrated false-discovery rate, not an arbitrary z cutoff (F6)
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • severity — not described in metric_meta; the dashboard will not claim semantics it was not given.

3Acceleration & emergencepriority metric family

“How fast is a scenario popping up” is not one quantity — it bundles curvature, a burst state, emergence from zero, and semantic novelty. All four are shown separately so an analyst can always ask why something flagged.

Why each cell flagged — the components, not just the composite

The composite emergence_score is a rank-combination, so a high score can come from very different evidence. Each panel below breaks a top-ranked cell into its four inputs, and shows explicitly which of them were NULL — a score built from two components is weaker evidence than one built from four, and the composite alone will not tell you which you have.

Smoothed acceleration by topic

One panel per topic: nine topics cannot be separated by hue in a black/white/gray/gold palette, so identity is carried by position instead. The gold ribbon under each axis marks days where the burst detector was in its burst state — an ordered state channel, not a fourth series.

Semantic novelty by topic

A topic can stay flat in volume while completely changing in content. This is the metric that sees that.

Days from first appearance to 5 posts/day

Speed of emergence from zero. Lower is faster.

  • Acceleration (raw) growth_t - growth_{t-1} (2nd difference of the log rate) units: log rate per day^2
    2nd differences double the noise — prefer accel_smooth
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • Smoothed acceleration 2nd derivative of the log rate from a CAUSAL Savitzky-Golay fit (trailing window 5, order 2, evaluated at the right edge) units: log rate per day^2
    the publishable acceleration. Deliberately causal: the textbook centred SG filter would consume future days (verified leak in stats_v2 self-check 'centered_savgol_does_leak'). Window-sensitive: corr(window 5, window 7) = 0.46, so the smoothing window is the main tuning risk.
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)
  • Burst state 1 = burst, 0 = baseline. Kleinberg 2-state Poisson HMM (Viterbi) where the series supports it, otherwise one-sided Poisson CUSUM; see burst_method per row units: 0/1
    run on raw counts (Kleinberg needs integer counts), so a rising collector ramp can inflate late-window burst states; the CUSUM path uses a trailing effort-adjusted baseline and does not have this problem
    gate: burst_support_ok (series n_days>=14 & mean(V)>=0.5)
  • Burst intensity Kleinberg: log p(x|lambda1) - log p(x|lambda0) clipped at 0. CUSUM: S_t / h (1.0 at the alarm boundary) units: nats (Kleinberg) or CUSUM units
    units differ between the two detectors — compare within burst_method only
    gate: burst_support_ok (series n_days>=14 & mean(V)>=0.5)
  • Semantic novelty 1 - cosine(day centroid, trailing 21-day topic centroid) over bge-m3 embeddings, trailing window EXCLUDES the current day units: cosine distance [0,2]
    NULL where embeddings are absent — youtube_video has none at all. Needs >=3 embedded posts that day and >=20 in the baseline; novelty_n / novelty_base_n carry the support.
    gate: novelty_n>=3 & novelty_base_n>=20
  • Days to threshold days from the series' first in-window appearance to the first day V>=5 units: days
    NULL until the threshold is actually crossed (no look-ahead)
    gate: NULL before crossing
  • Emergence score weighted mean of percentile ranks of surprise (0.40), accel_smooth (0.25), novelty (0.20) and -log1p(days_to_threshold) (0.15); >=2 components required, weights renormalised over the present ones units: rank in [0,1]
    the single alerting number for 'popping up'. Ranks are computed over the whole in-window panel, so the value is relative to this run's panel; emergence_flag additionally requires surprise_q < 0.05 (FDR-gated)
    gate: support_ok (baseline_days>=14 & baseline_mean>=0.5 & V>=3)

Volume rate per platform, with collection effort beneath

Effort is plotted as its own strip with its own baseline, never as a second y-axis on the same plot. A rise in raw V that is matched by a rise in effort is the scraper, not the world — this layout is what makes that visible instead of hiding it inside a ratio.

Growth by topic, most recent supported day

Diverging scale: gold for accelerating, clay for decelerating, neutral gray at zero — never a hue at the midpoint.

Momentum and 7-day growth by topic

Two series only, so gold carries the focal measure and gray the reference. Both are in the same units (log-rate), so they share one axis.

  • Volume count of distinct posts in the cell units: posts
    raw count; confounded by collector effort — use V_rate for trends
    gate: none (V is its own support)
  • Effort-normalised volume V / effort units: posts per active channel
    basis for all growth/acceleration metrics (F2 log-exposure offset)

    gate: floored at 1
  • Growth (log-rate) d/dt log((V+1)/effort) = log_rate_t - log_rate_{t-1} units: log rate per day
    effort-normalised (F2); the publishable growth number

    sensitive to span choice (corr 0.98 across 5/14 and 10/28 variants)

7Persistence

Whether a topic's attention sticks or dissipates, estimated from the whole series with a standard error instead of by discarding cells whose curve fit was weak.

AR(1) φ with ± 1 standard error

Gold dot = the point estimate, gray bar = ± 1 SE. Cells whose Ljung–Box p ≥ 0.05 are drawn in gray: there is no significant autocorrelation to model, so their φ and half-life describe noise. φ outside (0, 1) leaves the half-life genuinely undefined, and it is shown as “—” rather than filled in.

  • AR(1) persistence OLS phi from log_rate_t = c + phi*log_rate_{t-1} + e (LEVEL series) units: dimensionless
    SPEC DEVIATION, flagged for review: F7 specifies the AR(1) on the DIFFERENCED log rate, but differencing a stationary series forces phi<0, for which ln(0.5)/ln(phi) is undefined — it produced a half-life on only 4/39 series, reproducing the coverage weakness F7 set out to fix. The level fit is published here; the differenced fit is kept verbatim in diff_ar1_phi / diff_half_life_days.
    gate: n_obs>=12
  • Persistence half-life ln(0.5)/ln(ar1_phi), defined for 0<phi<1 units: days
    NULL when phi<=0 or phi>=1 (not mean-reverting); read with phi_se
    gate: 0<phi<1 and n_obs>=12
  • Volatility SD of the log-rate first differences units: log rate
    gate: n_obs>=2
  • Ljung-Box p Ljung-Box Q on the log-rate level, lags 1..7 units: p-value
    F8's primary evidence for autocorrelation, replacing the inverted v1 null-control criterion (D4)
    gate: n_obs>=8

10Virality & reach

Engagement expressed as an age-conditional percentile, because a parametric age normalisation did not fit — and reach labelled for what it actually is.

Virality percentile distribution per platform

Scrape lag ranges from 0.6 days to 150 days across platforms, so raw engagement comparisons measure observation delay, not virality. A percentile within (platform × age band) is distribution-free and age-honest.

Reach proxy

No follower or subscriber counts exist anywhere in the corpus. This is a proxy — views where available, otherwise a weighted engagement composite. For Telegram only, a channel's median views gives a defensible audience-size denominator (reach_audience_norm), which is still a proxy. Never present either as audience size.

  • Virality percentile EB-shrunk mean of per-post empirical percentiles of the platform amplification ratio within (platform x age band) units: percentile [0,1]
    F9: distribution-free and age-honest, replacing the low-R2 parametric age curve. Age = scrape_lag_days, bands 0/.5/1/2/4/7/14/30/90d, >=20 observations per band or it falls back to the platform. reddit is NULL (no engagement fields at all). Amplification ratio differs per platform, so only the percentile is cross-platform comparable.
    gate: non-null only where its own support count > 0
  • Reach (proxy) sum(views) where views exist, else sum(log1p(total engagement)) units: views or log-engagement
    A PROXY. No follower counts exist anywhere in the source data.
    gate: none
  • Audience-normalised reach mean over posts of views / median views of that channel (>=3 posts) units: ratio to channel median
    F10. Available only where views exist and the channel has >=3 view-bearing posts: 27 telegram channels and 160 youtube channels. Still a proxy.
    gate: channel median available

12Readability

The Flesch mean, and — inseparably — the share of posts that were long enough to score at all.

Readability mean and its coverage

YouTube comments have a median length of 59 characters with 36% under 40, which puts most of the corpus below the validity floor for any readability formula. The mean therefore describes a minority of posts, and the coverage meter is what tells you how small that minority is. A coverage figure below 20% is drawn in gray, not gold.

  • Readability mean of valid, language-appropriate readability scores, winsorised to [0,100] units: score
    coverage is low on short posts (13.7% on youtube_video, 48% on telegram) — always read alongside readability_coverage. Raw scores reach 1703 on degenerate text, hence the winsorisation.
    gate: read_n>0
  • Readability coverage share of posts in the cell with readability_valid units: fraction
    gate: none

14Hashtags⚠ ~15% coverage

Built, because hashtag alerting genuinely works where hashtags exist — and framed, because most of this corpus has none.

⚠ Coverage warning — spec §4

The hashtags field is populated for LinkedIn (502/924) and Instagram (237/337) and is empty for Telegram, YouTube, Reddit and Facebook. Mining inline #tags from post text adds only Telegram 1,883/14,595 (12.9%) and Reddit 66/2,107 (3.1%).

Hashtags cover roughly 15% of the corpus and ~13% of Telegram, the dominant source. A hashtag-keyed alerting system would be blind to about 85% of collected material.

1,272 distinct tags are in tag_dim. Key alerts on topic_id, which covers 79% of posts with the remainder explicitly unassigned; treat hashtag and keyword as secondary facets. Alerts in UI/data/metrics.json that came from a hashtag are tagged grain: "hashtag" so a consumer can weight them accordingly.

Known caveats carried through the whole dashboard

  • The corpus is live. It ingests while it is read, so any two queries run separately disagree. Every extract runs inside one REPEATABLE READ snapshot; production Postgres is read-only and nothing is ever written back.
  • The collector is a confounder. Telegram went 422 → 1,393 posts/day while active channels went 18 → 24. All trend metrics are computed on the effort-normalised log rate for exactly this reason; raw counts are kept beside them for transparency, not for trends.
  • Duplicates were a prerequisite, not hygiene. Telegram had 37% exact duplicate post_urls (Reddit 36%, Instagram 43% duplicate text), which inflated every Telegram volume figure by roughly 1.6× before dedup.
  • Sparsity is handled in the model, not by clipping. v1's robust-z reached |z| = 143 on cells of ≤ 2 posts because MAD collapsed toward zero. v2 uses a Negative-Binomial exceedance probability and a support gate, so a single post against a small baseline scores as the non-event it is.
  • Absent is not zero. Reddit has no engagement fields at all; Facebook has genuine zero likes on ~70% of posts. Collapsing those two into 0 would fabricate a finding, so engagement metrics are NULL where the field does not exist.
  • Half-life is aggregate-level. Rows are updated in place and history is overwritten, so every post is observed exactly once. Per-post engagement decay is not measurable from a single snapshot; an append-only engagement_snapshots table was seeded so the honest version becomes available later.
  • The corpus is multilingual. Telegram is 62% English, then ru/zh-cn/bn/uk/hi plus 2,626 unknown. English-only NLP would silently mishandle ~38%, so tone and toxicity use local multilingual models — and nothing leaves the network.

16Entity directoryverification: partial (precision unmeasured)

Who and what this corpus is actually about: people, organisations and places resolved from free text, kept even when a subtype can't be determined — unresolved is a category, not a silent drop.

What's in the directory

28,823 distinct entities were resolved from 107,257 NER + gazetteer mentions across the corpus (model: Babelscape/wikineural-multilingual-ner, hybrid with a 104-surface-form port/border/pipeline/railway gazetteer). Only 28.3% of mentions matched the gazetteer directly — the rest are model NER, kept even when unresolved (never dropped). The directory below ships entities seen in ≥2 distinct posts (6,797 of 28,823); singleton mentions are counted in the totals above but not rendered row-by-row.

  • LOCATION: 9,188 entities, 59,553 mentions
  • ORG: 11,448 entities, 28,941 mentions
  • PERSON: 8,187 entities, 18,763 mentions
Verification status: PARTIAL / precision not measured. The entity-extraction Implement stage finished after the independent verification pass had already run out of time waiting on it (it was still mid-NER at check time), so no stratified sample of real entity_mentions rows was read against source text — there is no measured precision figure for this table, only code-inspection risks: (1) several gazetteer surface forms are bare place names also used generically — Djibouti, Colombo, Gwadar — and get forced subtype="port" at subtype_confidence=1.0 regardless of context, a likely systematic false-positive source; (2) the script's own "~84% correct" figure comes from a 216-post prototype sample, not this 20,347-post run; (3) MAX_CHARS=1000 truncation silently misses entities later in long posts. Treat this directory as extracted-but-unaudited, not as verified ground truth.

9 risk categories and 12 thematic dimensions, multi-label per post via zero-shot embedding similarity to a category description — shown here, flagged here, because the fix is re-tuning, not silence.

⚠ Independent verification: UNSOUND — read the shape, not the label boundaries, as ground truth

Method is embedding_similarity_bge_m3_zeroshot: one flat cosine-similarity threshold (0.45) applied uniformly across all 9 risk and 12 theme categories, with no per-category calibration. A stratified 42-post sample (1 confident + 1 gray-zone example per category, hand-read against source text) found precision 29% strict / 45% lenient overall (43% / 62% on non-gray_zone rows, 14% / 24% on gray_zone=true rows — the flag is doing real, if crude, work). A separate ~20-post recall spot-check via keyword search (sanctions, insurgent, pipeline, belt-and-road, refugee) found clean misses: e.g. a Kirill Dmitriev quote that EU sanctions "do not work, cost >€3T" scored zero risk and zero theme tags; a Yemeni-missile-strike-on-a-named-pipeline post got the right risk tag but zero theme tags (missing energy_security).

Only 35.2% of the corpus (8,227 of 23,356 posts) carries any risk or theme label at all, and gray_zone=true on ~80–85% of all assigned labels. Do not treat risk_labels/theme_labels as ground truth without per-category threshold re-tuning — filter to gray_zone=false at minimum, and even that subset is only ~45–62% precise on lenient scoring.
